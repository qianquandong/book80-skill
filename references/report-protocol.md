# Book Distillation Report Protocol

Use this structure unless the user requests a different format.

## Header

- Title and author
- Edition or source filename
- Coverage: chapters/pages successfully processed
- Reading mode
- Confidence: high, medium, or low, with one-line reason

## 30-second brief

Answer in no more than 120 words total:

1. What problem does the book address?
2. What is its central thesis?
3. Who will benefit?
4. Is the original worth reading?

## 3-minute mental model

Include:

- 5–10 core ideas in ranked order
- an argument map showing how the ideas connect
- the three most important things to remember
- a one-paragraph account of what the book changes in the reader's thinking

## 10-minute deep brief

For each core idea, include:

1. **Author's claim** — faithful paraphrase `[Source: chapter/section/page]`
2. **Reasoning** — how the author reaches it
3. **Evidence** — strongest example, study, story, or mechanism
4. **Boundary** — where the claim may not apply
5. **Application** — concrete use, clearly labeled as analysis

## Chapter value map

Use a compact table:

| Chapter | Value | Contribution | Recommendation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Chapter name | 0–100 | What becomes understandable only after this chapter | Read / Skim / Skip |

Score value by novelty, explanatory power, practical utility, and evidence. Do not reward length.

## Critical assessment

Include:

- strongest contribution
- weakest or least-supported claim
- hidden assumptions
- meaningful counterargument
- important question left unanswered

Distinguish criticism from the author's own position.

## Action layer

Include only actions supported by the book:

- actions to try now
- conditions required
- warning signs or failure modes
- one small experiment for the next seven days

## Reading recommendation

Choose one:

- **Stop here** — the report captures the useful content for this reader
- **Read selected chapters** — name them and explain why
- **Read the full book** — explain what cannot be compressed without meaningful loss

## Evidence notation

Use:

- `[Source: p. 42]`
- `[Source: Chapter 3, “Feedback Loops”]`
- `[Source: EPUB spine 7, chapter-05.xhtml]`
- `[Analysis]`
- `[Application]`

Never place an analysis label on a claim and then attribute it to the author.
